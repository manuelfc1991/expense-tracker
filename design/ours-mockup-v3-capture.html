Capture, and a detail screen you can read

Three changes. A payment should be sortable the moment it happens, not hunted down later. The detail screen should show every category at once rather than hiding two thirds of them off the right edge. And a row should be able to carry a sentence about why, because in six months the amount will not remind you.

1 · The moment it happens

The heads-up notification already offers three guessed categories, but it is gone in seconds and it cannot rename anything. This is the same moment given room: the amount, who it looks like, the three best guesses, and — one tap further — the full list, a name and a note.

It appears only when the app is open. A sheet that steals focus mid-payment would be worse than useless; if the app is in the background the existing notification still does its job, and this is waiting when you next open it.

Fifteen categories in a three-column grid: all of them visible, none hidden off an edge. Paid to is new — the account that received the money, which is what a name can be attached to.

2 · Why the grid, not the strip

The detail screen scrolls its categories horizontally today, which shows five of fifteen. The two thirds off-screen are invisible: there is no scrollbar on Android and nothing indicates the row continues, so a category you have never scrolled to might as well not exist. That is how a ledger ends up with everything in Other.

A grid is taller and worth it. Fifteen chips at three across is five rows — about the height of the amount above them — and every option is a single tap from wherever your thumb already is. The detail screen is not a place people linger; it is a place they fix one thing and leave.

3 · The note

Optional, and never in the way. Empty, it is one dim line saying "Add a note". Filled, it sits under the amount where it explains it. It is the only field in this app that holds something the bank could never have sent, which makes it the only defence against a row that made sense in August and is a mystery by December.

It syncs like everything else, so a note written on one phone answers the other's question too. Never used for matching or categorising — a note is for the household to read, not for the app to interpret.

What this does not change

The heads-up notification stays exactly as it is. It works when the app is closed, which is most of the time, and its three one-tap chips are already the fastest path when the guess is right. This sheet is for when it is not — and for the naming and the note, neither of which fits in a notification.
