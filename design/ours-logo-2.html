Ours — logo studies, second set

Fifteen more, none repeating the first six — twelve marks, then three built on two hands. Each is shown filled on the accent, drawn on ink, and at 48px and 28px — because the launcher and the notification tray are where it actually lives, not at 96. The right-hand pair is the same mark under a circular crop, which is what a good half of Android launchers will do to it.

The twelve

07

The fold

A statement with its corner turned. The only mark here that says paper outright, which is the whole conceit of the app — everything else is geometry that happens to be printed.

08

Two into one

Two figures above, one total below. Literally what the app does with two people's money, and it survives being 28px better than anything with a curve in it.

09

Equal shares

An equals sign, read as a household rather than as arithmetic. Two rules of identical weight: neither person's column is the important one.

10

The ledger row

The app's actual signature: a name on the left, a figure flush right, twice. It is the one mark here that could only belong to this app.

11

The ring, halved

One O of OURS, drawn as two arcs that do not quite touch. Two people making one round number; the gaps are what stop it reading as a loading spinner.

12

Roof and rule

A household over a ledger line. The most literal mark of the set and the most immediately readable — you know what it is before you know what the app is.

13

Held between

Two brackets and the space they enclose. What is ours is the gap, not the marks — which is either the cleverest thing here or the least legible, depending on the size.

14

In and out

Money arriving and money leaving, the two things every row in the app is. Reads instantly; risks looking like a transfer utility rather than a ledger.

15

The share

A percent mark with its slash turned into a rule. Says *proportion* — which is what the summary screen is about — without drawing a single pie chart.

16

The split

One line becoming two. The gesture of a shared expense, drawn once — and the only mark here with any movement in it.

17

The month

Four days of a daily chart. Lifted straight from the Home screen, so the icon and the first thing you see after tapping it are the same drawing.

18

The corner

A rule turning a right angle, with the figure it carries. The quietest mark of the twelve; also the one that reads as a mark rather than a picture at 28px.

Two hands

One constraint before the drawings: a hand with fingers does not survive 28px. At the size this icon actually lives, four fingers become one grey smudge with a notch. So every hand here is a silhouette or an arc — a palm implied by its outline, never drawn in detail. The disc in the middle of each is the money; it is what stops two arcs from reading as a pair of brackets.

19

Cupped

Two palms making a bowl, thumbs up at the ends, with a note laid across the top. The most elegant of the hand studies and the least literal — at 28px it reads as a basket as often as it reads as hands.

20

Cupped, solid

The same bowl as a mass, split down the middle so it reads as two hands rather than one dish. The heaviest mark in either set — it keeps its silhouette at 28px and under a circular crop, which most of these do not.

21

Palm to palm

Two hands proper, thumbs out, a note held between them. The only one of the three that reads as *hands* and not as a container — and the price is that it is the least refined. The thumb is the single detail that survives shrinking, so it is the only one drawn.

What the launcher will do to it

An adaptive icon is handed to the launcher as a square and masked to whatever shape that launcher likes — circle, squircle, rounded square, square. A mark that only works in one of them is a mark that looks broken on somebody's phone. These three survive all four.

Where I would land

MarkCase for itCase against
10 · The ledger row The only one that could belong to no other app. Name left, figure right, twice — it is the Home screen, shrunk. Busiest of the twelve. The third rule has to go at 28px.
12 · Roof and rule Legible before you know what the app is. A household over a ledger line, and it holds at 28px and under every mask. House icons are common. It says home more loudly than it says money.
08 · Two into one Says the actual idea — two people, one total — in three rectangles. Nothing to break at any size. Reads as a bar chart until someone explains it.
20 · Cupped, solid The warmest mark of the set, and the only one about the people rather than the paperwork. Solid enough to keep its shape at 28px and under a circular mask. Cupped hands are the house style of every charity and savings app there is.

Among the hands, take 20 if you want the better icon and 21 if you want it to be read as hands. 19 is the prettiest at full size and the vaguest at launcher size.

One caution worth stating plainly: hands are the house style of every charity, insurance and savings app there is, and they speak a different visual language from the rest of this app, which is hairlines, mono type and printed rules. That is not a reason not to use them — a household ledger has a decent claim on the gesture — but the icon would be the one thing in Ours that does not look like the rest of Ours.

My pick overall is still 10, the ledger row, with 12 as the safe choice if you want something a stranger can name at a glance. Both beat my earlier recommendation (06, the shared line) for the same reason: two dots over a rule is elegant and says nothing, and at 28px it is three grey marks.
