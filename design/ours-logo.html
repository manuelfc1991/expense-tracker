Ours — logo studies

Six directions. Each is shown at 96px, at 48px — the size a launcher actually draws — and at 28px, because an icon that only works large is not an icon.

The constraint worth stating first: this app already has a mark. OURS in tracked monospace sits at the top of every screen, and the whole visual language is hairlines, printed rules and tabular figures. A logo that ignores that is a second identity competing with the first.

01

The wordmark, cropped

48 · 28

The first two letters of the wordmark already on every screen, over the accent rule that runs under every section header. Nothing invented.

Strongest continuity, weakest distinctiveness. At 28px "OU" reads as a truncation rather than a mark, and two-letter icons are the most crowded shelf on any home screen.

02

Two rules, one ledger

48 · 28

Three statement lines: two full, one short. The accent one is the total. This is literally what the app looks like — a printed statement of ruled lines.

Survives 28px better than anything else here, and it is unmistakably a ledger. But it says nothing about ours: it could be any finance app.

03

Two circles, one overlap

48 · 28

Two people, one shared middle. Close to what the app ships with today, drawn properly: equal weights, one ring in the accent so the pair reads as two rather than as a blur.

Says "ours" more directly than any other option. The risk is that interlocking rings are the most-used mark in the world — you will meet it on other icons, and the overlap muddies below about 32px.

04

The budget ruler

48 · 28

The ruler under the month's figure, which is the single most distinctive element in this app — nothing else on a phone looks like it. Half spent, half left.

The most "this app and no other" of the six. It also collapses fastest: at 28px the ticks merge into a smear, and it needs at least a 4-tick version drawn separately for small sizes.

05

Rupee, ruled

48 · 28

The rupee sign in the app's own mono, underlined by the accent rule. Unambiguous about what the app is for, and unambiguously Indian.

Clear at every size and instantly legible — but a currency glyph is the default choice for a money app, and it would sit on the home screen looking like a banking app rather than a household one.

06

The shared line

48 · 28

Two people above one line. The line is the ledger they share — the same hairline that separates every row in the app — and the two marks sit on it equally.

My pick. It says the thing the app is actually about, it is built from elements already on screen, and it is the only option here that stays legible at 28px while still meaning something. Two solid dots do not merge the way two rings do.

HOW TO CHOOSE

Look at the 28px column first and ignore the large ones. That is the size your launcher draws, and it is where four of these six fall apart. Then ask which one you would recognise on a crowded home screen without reading a label — an icon's whole job is to be found, not admired.
