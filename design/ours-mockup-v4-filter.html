Filtering the Activity screen

The category filter is a horizontal strip of eighteen chips. Three and a half of them fit on the screen, Android draws no scrollbar, and the rest are not off-screen so much as invisible. This is four ways out of that, drawn against your real August.

0 · What is actually there

Every category exists as a chip, in enum order, with a full label and an icon — Food & Dining, Transport & Fuel. Wide chips and eighteen of them is the whole problem: you swipe past four before reaching anything, and there is no indication how much further there is to go.

1 · Only what the month has  — recommended

The strip stops being a list of every category the app knows and becomes a list of what is in front of you, most-used first, each carrying its count. Seven chips instead of eighteen, short labels, no icons — and every chip is guaranteed to return rows, because it only exists if rows exist.

Untagged leads, in amber. It is the biggest group and the only one that is asking for something. When it reaches zero the chip disappears on its own, the way the Sort card on Home already does.

2 · Once a filter is on

The chips do their job and then get out of the way. A chosen filter collapses the whole row into one line that names it, counts it, and carries the way out — because after the tap the question is no longer "which category" but "am I still filtered", and that deserves to be unmissable rather than a blue chip somewhere in a row.

It also hands back two rows of height to the list, which is what you came to read.

3 OF 19 in the corner, and a total for the filtered set. A filtered screen that still says "19 entries" is lying about what you are looking at.

Why not just leave the chosen chip highlighted? Because it scrolls away. The current strip can be filtered to Rent, scrolled back to the left, and then it looks exactly like an unfiltered screen with a short list — which is the single worst thing a filter can do, since it makes missing data look like absent data.

The collapsed line cannot scroll away. It sits where the chips were and it does not move.

3 · More — the full list, when the month does not have it

Occasionally you want a category the month contains none of: checking whether anything landed under Health, or hunting a row you know you miscategorised. More opens the same grid the detail and add sheets already use, with the empty ones dimmed but still tappable — the count is the answer to that question, so it is worth showing rather than hiding.

Eighteen cells, all of them on one screen, no scrolling anywhere. This is the same component as the picker you already approved — the app should not have two ways of drawing a list of categories.

Why a sheet and not this grid inline on the screen? Drawn permanently above the list it costs six rows of height on every visit, to answer a question you ask once a week. The strip's sin is being hard to reach; an always-open grid's sin is being always open. The wrapped row in section 1 is the middle: short, complete for the common case, one tap from the rest.

Show 3 rather than Apply. The button says what will be on the other side of it, so a filter that would empty the screen says Show 0 and you do not have to tap it to find out.

4 · The one I would not build

For completeness, since it is the obvious first idea: make the strip scroll better — keep all eighteen, shorten the labels, drop the icons, add a fade at the edge.
