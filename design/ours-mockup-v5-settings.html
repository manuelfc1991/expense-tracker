Organising Settings

Settings is eleven sections and about two dozen controls in one scroll. Nothing in it is wrong; the problem is that things which belong together are far apart, and the three switches that can silently break the app are buried among the ones that cannot.

0 · What is there now

In order: Household, Sync, Message scanning, Notifications, Where expenses come from, Tracking, Appearance, Privacy, When a payment happens, Updates, About. Reaching the bottom takes about a dozen swipes.

THREE PROBLEMS
WhatWhere it hurts
One subject, split in two Notifications (section 4) and When a payment happens (section 9) are the same subject — what Ours does the moment money moves — with five sections between them. Turning the popup on without noticing notifications are off is a thing you can currently do.
One subject, split in three Message scanning, Where expenses come from and Tracking all answer "which payments become rows". They are separated by an unrelated section.
Filed under the wrong thing Auto-assign rules, Parser tester and Delete requests sit under "Where expenses come from". None of them is a source. Delete requests is a household approval queue and belongs with the household.

There is also a naming split nobody would notice but everybody feels: half the sections are nouns (Sync, Tracking, Privacy, About) and half are sentences (Where expenses come from, When a payment happens). Scanning a list of headings works when they are the same kind of thing.

1 · Five groups, one scroll  — recommended

Eleven sections become five, by putting each control next to the question it answers rather than next to the code that implements it. Nothing is removed and nothing moves behind a new screen — the same settings, in an order you can predict.

Every heading is a noun phrase for a thing you own: the household, the sync, the money coming in, the moment of payment, the app itself.

Notification and popup are now one panel, because they are one decision made twice. Each shows its own permission where it is, rather than in a separate section you have to know to look at.

The banner at the top is the real change. Every failure this app has actually had was a permission that was off while everything looked fine — notifications never requested, notification access never granted, and now the overlay permission, which Android can revoke at any time without telling anyone.

A settings screen that only shows state when you scroll to the right section will not catch that. One line at the top, shown only when something is wrong, and absent entirely when nothing is, means the answer to "is Ours working" is on screen the moment you open it.

Each row carries its own summary, so the index is a status report as well as a menu.

Why I would still pick section 1. An index is the right shape when settings are numerous and unrelated — a phone's own settings app. Here there are five groups and about two dozen controls, which is one comfortable scroll, and the cost of the index is that everything is now two taps and a screen transition away, including the things you actually revisit: the sync status, the tracking date, the popup switch.

It also splits the prose. Half of what makes this screen usable is the sentence under each control explaining what it costs you — those sentences are the reason the panels look the way they do. Behind five subpages, nobody reads them a second time.

Worth taking from it anyway: the summary line under each heading. Section 1 already puts 2 PEOPLE, 2 MIN AGO and the version on their section rules, which is the same idea without the navigation.

3 · Two smaller things

The invite QR should be behind a tap. It is a large white square near the top of the screen, needed exactly once per person, and it is the reason Household is the tallest section in the app. As a row that opens the code and QR, it costs one tap on the day you use it and nothing on every other day.

Developer mode should not be under About. It is unlocked from there — seven taps on the version, then the household code — which is right, but its rows then appear in the middle of a panel about what version you are running. They belong in their own panel at the very bottom, below the encryption note, where the screen has already ended for everyone who has not unlocked it.
