Ours · v7 · August 2026

Statement, on Material 3

A complete redesign of Ours — the Android expense tracker that parses Indian bank and UPI SMS and keeps two phones in one household in step. Every screen, every sheet, every dialog, every state, in light and dark, with the design system behind it.

What this is not: a replacement for the app’s visual identity. The governing idea — a bank SMS is a machine-printed statement line — is the best thing about this product and v7 keeps it intact. What v7 replaces is the system underneath: MD3 colour roles instead of bespoke names, a 4dp grid instead of 15/7/9/11/13dp, real touch targets instead of tappable 9dp glyphs, and the three UI states the app currently has no design for at all.

What changed, and why

Twenty-three changes, ordered by how much they matter. Every one came out of reading the app’s own source or docs/REVIEW.md — several are problems the code comments already admit to, and three are the money-model findings that review rates highest.

Accessibility — five systematic failures

#Nowv7
1 Tap targets are the glyph itself: back arrows 16dp, bill dismiss 13dp, detail delete 15dp, and the rule-chip delete at 9dp — the smallest target in the app, on a destructive action. 48dp minimum everywhere, 40dp only where two controls are unavoidably adjacent, never below the 24dp WCAG floor. The glyph stays small; the target does not.
2 Accent-as-text is 3.80:1 on near-black — and accent text is used for “Sort”, “Edit”, “See all”, “Put back” on nearly every screen. The role is split: primary for text at 11.16:1, primaryFixed for fills. This is what MD3’s fixed roles are for.
3 The 9sp micro-label, which captions every figure on every screen — below MD3’s labelSmall floor. 11sp floor for all text. Tracking carries the “caption” signal, not smallness.
4 Light captions at 4.25:1 and Shopping at 2.90:1 — both failures the source comments name and accept. Every role re-derived and measured. Worst text pair in v7: 5.00:1.
5 Food and Education are near-identical to a deuteranope; three to four category pairs collapse in each theme. The source states this and accepts it. Lightness is allowed to vary, which restores separation once hue is gone. 0 of 66 pairs collapse, in both themes, under all three dichromacies.

States — three of five were missing

#Nowv7
6 No offline design at all. Sync state is a pill on Home and nothing else. A ribbon under the app bar that distinguishes what is certainly current (this phone’s own SMS) from what may be missing (the other phone, since a named time). Figures are never hidden — they are correct, just possibly incomplete.
7 No error surface outside a red sentence inside a Settings disclosure. A notice that names the cause, offers one fix, and leaves the data visible beneath it.
8 Loading is a skeleton on Home and the words “Reading the month” everywhere else. One skeleton system, shaped like the content, at the real row height. Nothing under 200ms shows anything — a flash is worse than a wait.
9 Empty states are one line of grey text. Icon, a sentence about the world, a body line about why that is fine, and an action only when there is genuinely one to take.

Interaction hazards

#Nowv7
10 The bulk-selection toolbar is four identical 9sp uppercase words — All · Categorize · Delete · Done — with Delete third, between two harmless ones. What it destroys can include hand-made entries that exist in no backup. 48dp icon buttons, Delete at the far end in the error colour, away from where a thumb reaching for “done” lands. Confirm dialog and undo both kept.
11 The add button vanishes the moment the list moves, so the only way to add an expense is unavailable during the gesture people use while looking for it. Shrinks to the 40dp variant on scroll-down, restores at rest. The list keeps its bottom padding, so the original concern — never covering an amount — still holds.
12 Two “Delete it / Keep it” text links 18dp apart, doing opposite irreversible things. Two 40dp buttons with clear space, and an undo on approval.

Findability

#Nowv7
13 The untagged count lives only on Home, so a household with 94 unsorted rows has no idea until it happens to look. A badge on the Activity tab, visible from everywhere, with a spoken label.
14 Search exists only on Activity, so finding a payee means changing tab first. In the app bar on Home too, and Settings gets its own — a 1,669-line settings screen is not navigable by scrolling.
15 The bottom bar has no active indicator and 9sp uppercase labels — the hardest text in the app to read, on the thing you navigate by. An MD3 indicator in secondaryContainer, so the tab is unmistakable and the accent stays unique to actions. Labels 12sp sentence case.
16 Summary is one 1,000dp scroll holding three different questions. Three tabs: Month, Accounts, Committed. Export moves to an app-bar icon from the very bottom of the longest screen in the app.
17 Settings ships both layouts with a chip behind the developer unlock to choose — indecision shipped as a preference. One layout: the index, because five rows that report their own state is a status report as well as a menu.

Gaps that are functional, not cosmetic

#Nowv7
18 The add sheet has no date or time field, so a cash lunch entered in the evening lands in the wrong hour — and entered after midnight, the wrong day’s subtotal. A When row: Now / Earlier today / Pick a date, with Now as the default so the common case still costs nothing. MD3 date and time pickers are specified.
19 No response to window size at all — one 360dp layout everywhere, including a rotated Android 16 handset and any foldable. Three window classes: bottom bar under 600dp, navigation rail above it, two-pane list-plus-detail at 840dp — which the detail screen already is.
20 The widget prints a bare percentage: “68% of ₹32K”. The ruler, which answers “am I fine” in one look. The whole point of a widget is glancing rather than reading.

The money model — from docs/REVIEW.md

Three of that review’s four findings are design problems as much as code problems, so v7 answers them. The fourth — the missing test floor over ui and data/repo — is not something a mockup can fix, and is left where the review put it.

#Nowv7
21 The budget has no idea what day it is. On the morning the review was written Home read 74% used, ₹9,710 left, meter green — on the 6th of a 31-day month, where straight-line pace is about 19%. Nothing in the budget path takes the date as an input, and BudgetAlerter’s flat listOf(100, 80) fires identically on the 3rd and the 28th. A pacing line: “A DAY FROM HERE · ₹292 · 25 days left, after ₹2,400 still committed”. It paces the discretionary money — (budgetLeft − stillCommitted) ÷ daysRemaining — so rent on the 1st never triggers it, and the alert fires on the daily figure rather than on a percentage. Three states plus two edge cases are specified on the Home page.
22 A refund is income, and the purchase it undoes still counts as spending. Every credit that is not a maturing investment becomes Income, so a ₹2,000 return leaves the ledger with a ₹2,000 debit and a ₹2,000 credit. Net worth is right; spending is overstated by ₹2,000 and the budget has been charged for a purchase that was undone. A deliberate “This is a refund” action on a credit, which asks which purchase it cancels — manual and auditable, because matching on amount is the trap that has already bitten this household. Four-step flow on the Flows page, including how the pair reads afterwards and where it lands in “Not counted”.
23 Two timezones in one app. Money is bucketed in Asia/Kolkata; every date the interface formats uses systemDefault() — including the tracking-start picker. Both phones are in IST today, so nothing is visibly wrong until one of them travels. One zone for both, stated as a design rule rather than an implementation detail: it is what makes a day’s subtotal equal the sum of the rows printed under it. The full date format table is on the Design system page.

What v7 deliberately does not change

The printed-statement row, with its shared right-hand amount column and no ₹ per line. The ruler as discrete ticks. One accent and one primary action per screen. Hairlines instead of elevation. Ranked bars instead of a donut. Label above value. The absence of swipe actions. “Say what is missing, never no data.” Every one of those is a decision the app got right, several of them after getting it wrong first, and the source comments record why. A redesign that discarded them would be a different product wearing the same name.

Implementing it

Ordered so that each step is shippable on its own and none of them requires the next one.

StepChangeTouches
1Tokens. Rename OursColors fields to the MD3 roles, add primary/primaryFixed, drop in the measured values and the new category maps. ui/theme/Color.kt, Theme.kt. Mechanical, and it fixes failures 2, 4 and 5 on its own.
2Type. 9sp → 11sp on MicroLabelStyle and labelSmall; align the rest of the scale. Type.kt. Expect 4–6px of extra height per captioned figure; the mockups are drawn at the new size and still fit.
3Targets. Wrap every tappable glyph in a 48dp IconButton. Every screen, but each edit is one line. This is the highest-value change in the set.
4Spacing and shape. 15dp → 16dp edge; the 7/9/11/13dp paddings onto the 4dp grid; OursShapes onto the MD3 scale. Type.kt shapes, and the EDGE constant each screen declares — which should become one shared token rather than eight copies.
5App bar and nav. One OursTopBar with title / back / selection variants; nav indicator and badge. ui/nav/Navigation.kt, plus deleting the hand-rolled header row from each of the sixteen screens that has one.
6The three missing states. An offline ribbon, an error notice, one skeleton system. New components, then wiring per screen. The only step that needs new view-model state: each screen needs to know “last synced” and “last error”.
7Commitment-aware pacing. The daily-allowance line on Home, and BudgetAlerter firing on it rather than on flat percentages. No new data — committedRemaining(), RecurringDetector and Affordability all exist. The review estimates about a day, and rates it first of everything on its own list.
8The refund action. A field on both transactions, a picker sheet, and totalSpent/totalReceived honouring it. The model change is small; the interface is the part that needed designing, and it is specified. Syncs as an ordinary last-write-wins field.
9One timezone constant. An hour, and it closes a trap rather than a bug. Worth doing before anyone travels.
10Structure. Summary tabs, Settings search, the When row on the add sheet. Feature work. Independent of everything above.
11Window classes. Rail and two-pane. The largest single change, and the one with the least urgency — worth doing when there is a tablet or a foldable in the household to test on.

Two things to check before touching the app

Release builds must use ours-release.jks. A build signed with any other key cannot install over what is on the phones, and the only way past that is an uninstall — which destroys manual entries and hand-made category corrections that exist in no backup and cannot be rebuilt from SMS. If keystore.properties is missing, the build still succeeds and silently produces an unsigned APK.

And run Gradle from the repository root. From a subdirectory it silently does nothing, which has already led once to a stale APK being installed and a fix reported as shipped when it was not.
