13 · Paid from

Purpose. Say which account a hand-added payment came out of.

Why it is needed. addManual writes no account and no bank, and accountBalances() begins by discarding every row that has neither (filter { !it.accountTail.isNullOrBlank() || it.bank != null }). So a hand-added payment is not merely unattributed — it is invisible to the Accounts tab entirely. The parser fills this in from the message; the sheet that exists precisely for payments no bank messaged about is the one place nothing fills it in at all.

On the entryThe same row the parser fills for an SMS, in the same place, editable. A hand-added row and a parsed one read identically.

  • Components Sheet · money input · chip row (single-select) · category grid · filled and outline buttons. Nothing new — the chip row is the one already used for When and Counts as.
  • Where the options come from accountBalances(), which already knows every account the household transacts through, plus two answers it cannot know: Cash and Not sure. Both are real answers and neither should be a blank.
  • What it must not do Selecting an account must not move that account's balance. Balances in this app are quoted, never derived — “the app never sees a cash deposit or a silent interest credit, so a balance computed by adding up transactions would drift from the truth and never find its way back”. Attribution answers which account, not how much is in it.
  • Interactions
    • One tap sets it; there is no confirm step, because it is one field of a form that already has Save.
    • Chips scroll horizontally when a household has more accounts than fit.
    • “Not sure” is not a failure state — it is stored as unknown and the Accounts tab reports it as unknown, the same way it already reports a balance nobody has quoted.
  • Improved
    • Hand-added rows stop being invisible to Accounts.
    • The capture sheet gets the same row, so a payment caught from a notification is attributable too.

14 · Credit cards in Accounts

Purpose. Add a credit card alongside bank accounts, and show what is owed on it rather than what is in it.

The rule this hangs on. A card balance is a debt. The Accounts tab totals “what is left”, and Affordability spends against it. Adding ₹4,200 of card debt to that total would tell the household it has ₹4,200 more to spend than it does — the exact opposite of the truth. So cards are a second block with its own total, and its own word: owed, never left.

Adding the cardThe limit is optional and buys one thing: “still free”. Without it the card still works, it just cannot say how much room is left.

  • Never summed together Two totals, never one. Affordability reads “what is left”; the owed figure is reported beside it and never enters it. An account with no balance recorded is already counted-and-reported rather than summed as zero, and a card follows the same rule.
  • The card bill stops double-counting This is the part that is not cosmetic. Category.CARD_PAYMENT counts as spending today, deliberately: “on this ledger not one of 460 rows was an individual card purchase, so the bill is the only record”. The SuperCard breaks that — its purchases arrive from UTKSPR and have been read since 1 August. Registering a card here is what tells the app that this card's purchases are recorded, so this card's bill must not be. The ICICI card, whose purchases still never arrive, keeps counting its bill. Per card, not per category — which is exactly why it belongs in an account the household creates rather than in a rule in the code.
  • Components Chooser sheet · text fields · chip row · notice · filled button · the existing accounts table with a second block and its own tape header.
  • NavSummary → Accounts → Add an account → kind → form. Back at any point keeps nothing.

15 · Possible payments

Purpose. Never silently miss a payment, whoever sent the message — without silently counting one that is not a payment.

Why it is a queue and not automatic. The request was to track any message carrying a payment, debit or credit keyword. That exact rule was built and measured against this household's 2,810 real messages first. Ninety-nine sender headers were unrecognised; six of them wrote something with an amount, a settled verb and a masked number, and none of the six was a bank — an EPF passbook line, an Amazon Pay balance, a fuel loyalty receipt, a Myntra gift card and two trading spams. The rule read the EPF line as ₹61,989 of income and accepted an SMS advertising a shortened link.

So the catch is kept wide and the counting is what waits. Everything that looks like a payment is captured; anything from a sender we cannot vouch for lands here first, and one tap settles it forever.

EmptyA fact, not “no data”. Reached only by going looking, since the card on Home is gone when this is empty.

  • What gets caught An amount, a settled verb (debited / credited / paid / spent / received / withdrawn) and an account, card or reference number. Known senders go straight to the ledger as they do today. Unknown ones come here. A sender that names a bank we already know (“-Federal Bank”, “-Utkarsh SFBL”) skips the queue — that is already built.
  • One tap, forever “Yes, my bank” writes the header into the shared sender rules, so it travels to the other phone over the sheet and neither of you is ever asked again. “Not a payment” writes the same rule with an empty value, which is this store's tombstone — the sender is not re-proposed on its next message.
  • Not in the total until answered The rows exist but are excluded from every figure, exactly as a row before the tracking-start cutoff is. The screen says so in the first line, because a number that is “nearly your spending” is worse than one that is plainly not.
  • Why grouped by sender Ninety-nine unknown headers produced 1,386 messages on the real phone. Asked message by message that is 1,386 decisions; asked sender by sender it is at most ninety-nine, and in practice the six that carry an amount at all.
  • Improved
    • A bank that changes its header is caught the same day instead of going unnoticed for months — the FEDSMS and UTKSPR failures both surface here on the first message.
    • The decision is reversible: Settings → Auto-assign rules lists sender rules alongside merchant ones, and clearing one puts the sender back in the queue.
