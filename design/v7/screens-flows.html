Flows and child screens

The twelve screens that are not tabs: onboarding, the entry detail, the jobs (Sort, Trash, Delete requests, marking a refund), the tools (Rules, Parser, Sheet setup, QR), Backup, and the lock gate. These keep the tab bar hidden — they are jobs you are part-way through, and a tab tap mid-job loses the work. The one exception is the entry detail, and the reason is given below.

Purpose

The only reason to open a single row is to find out whether the app got it right. So the amount is the hero — it is the thing you came to check — and everything under it is provenance: which bank, which account, and the original message the parser read.

  • Paise This is the one screen that shows them. It is where you reconcile against a bank statement, so the exact figure matters; every list rounds, and every total that sits over rows prints them.
  • Keeps the tabs A row opens by tapping a list, so it reads as a place inside Activity rather than somewhere you were taken — and people reach for Home. With the bar gone the only way out is a back arrow in the far corner, and tapping where Home should be does nothing at all, which is indistinguishable from the app having frozen.

  • Interactions
    • Tap the payee → rename. Banks often send no payee at all — a Kerala Gramin UPI debit names only the destination account — so the person who made the payment is the only one who can say who it went to. The dialog offers to remember the account, which names every payment to it, past and future.

08 · Sort

Purpose

Turn a chore nobody starts into a handful of decisions a person can actually make. Ninety-four unsorted rows is unstartable; the same rows grouped by who was paid is about six decisions — and “Keecheril St is food” is knowledge you have, where “transaction 47 of 94” is not.

  • Every answer is a rule Assigning a group also writes the rule, so the same payee never asks again — and the card says so: “Applies to all 12, and every future one.”
  • Half-made decisions The leading guess is the only filled chip and the rest are outlines, so the one-tap path is obvious at a glance. Ninety-four rows become six decisions only if each decision is already half made. The avatar is tinted with that leading guess rather than left grey — the colour is a preview of what tapping the first chip would do.
  • Unknown payee When the bank named nobody, the card says so in plain language — “The bank named nobody, so it’s counted as spending for now” — not “uncategorised debit”, which is a description of the database rather than of what happened.

    “It was spending” is the filled chip, and this page used to say the opposite. The earlier draft called the row “held out of spending until you decide” and recommended “Moving money”. Both were wrong against the money model: a debit with no named payee lands in Transfers, Transfers counts, and on the real ledger 83 of 85 such rows were money sent to other people. So the row is in the total from the moment it arrives, and the easiest tap must not be the one that takes it out. Tapping “Moving money” writes Ours — never Transfers, which is the category the row already has, and which made the chip do nothing at all.
  • Components App bar with counter · ruler as progress · group card · category avatar · chips · amount column · category sheet
  • Interactions
    • A settled group fades to 45% rather than disappearing. Rows vanishing under your thumb makes the list feel unstable and costs you the chance to notice a mistake.
    • The ruler at the top fills as you go, which is the whole reward.
    • “More” opens the full grid — the same grid as the detail screen, the add sheet and the filter, so the categories are in the same place every time.
  • NavFrom Home’s action card and from Activity’s bar. Back returns.
  • Improved
    • Undo on the last assignment, so a mis-tap on a 12-payment group does not need finding again in Activity.
    • A finished screen offers the way onward (“Back to Activity”) instead of a bare tick.

Purpose

A delete is the one change nobody can inspect afterwards — an edit leaves a value to disagree with, a deletion leaves nothing at all. So a member’s delete becomes a request, and the owner decides.

  • Still counted Rows here remain in every total until they are approved, because a request is not yet a decision and the headline must not move on the strength of one.
  • Where it surfaces A notice on Home, above the bills — somebody is waiting on an answer, and until it comes their delete looks to them like a button that does nothing. A pill buried in Settings was not enough: nobody opens Settings to check for a question they have not been told was asked.
  • Improved
    • The two actions become 40dp buttons with clear space between them. They were adjacent 11sp text labels with opposite, irreversible effects.
    • Approving offers an Undo, and the row still lands in Trash for 30 days.
    • Rejecting tells the asker, so their delete stops looking broken.

Empty

Nothing waiting on you

If someone asks to remove an entry, it appears here and on Home.

A tick, not the ledger’s receipt glyph. Most empty states in this app are an empty month; an approvals queue with nothing in it is the opposite — it is finished.

11 · Auto-assign rules

Purpose

The rule the app already learns silently every time you sort a payee is the same rule you can write here by hand. This screen exists so that behaviour stops being invisible: you can see what the app decided on your behalf, correct it, and add the ones it will never guess — a landlord’s name means nothing to a parser and means “rent” to you every month.

  • Yours vs shipped Rules you wrote are outlined in the accent; the ones that came with the app are plain. So “did I do this, or did it come like that?” is answerable at a glance.
  • Said out loud Two things surprise people, so they are stated rather than left to be discovered: matching ignores case and matches anywhere in the name, so “reliance” catches “Reliance Smart” too; and money coming in is never matched, because credits are always Income.
  • Improved
    • The chip’s delete becomes a 24dp target inside a 32dp chip. It was a 9dp glyph — the smallest tap target in the app, on a destructive action.
    • Deleting a rule offers Undo, because a rule is invisible once gone and you would only notice next month.
    • A search field, since a household accumulates dozens across sixteen collapsed sections.
    • Each section says how many rows the rule currently matches, so a rule that does nothing is visible as such.

16 · Marking a refund NEW

The second high-severity finding in docs/REVIEW.md, and the only one that needs a genuinely new interaction. Every credit that is not a maturing investment becomes Income (Categorizer.kt:25), and spending counts debits only. So return a ₹2,000 item and the ledger holds a ₹2,000 debit and a ₹2,000 credit: net worth is right, spending is overstated by ₹2,000, and the budget has been charged for a purchase that was undone. Near the cap, the app tells the household to stop when it need not.

Why this cannot be automatic

Matching on amount is the obvious idea and it is the trap. Two ₹2,000 movements in a month are far more often two real payments than a purchase and its refund — and this household has already been bitten by a matcher that was too eager: two ₹10,000 movements a minute apart, an FD maturing and rent paid to a person, both real. DedupeTimeTest and MixedReferenceDedupeTest exist because of exactly that.

This is also a different thing from the reversal handling that already works. SmsParser rejects “reversed”, “reversal”, “refunded to your”, “cancelled” — that is the bank reporting a payment that never completed, and refusing to record it is correct. A merchant refund arriving three days later is a genuine, separate credit, and nothing catches it.

So it is a deliberate, auditable, one-tap-to-undo action: the same pattern the app already uses for every other judgement it refuses to make on the household’s behalf.

4 · How the pair reads afterwardsThe purchase keeps its place in the day it happened, with its amount struck through and the day’s subtotal already excluding it. Both sides join “Not counted”, which is where this app already puts real money that is not spending.

  • Where offered On the detail screen of any credit that is not already linked and is not a maturing investment. As a dismissible notice, not a permanent row — a salary needs no asking about, and a prompt on every credit forever is a tax.
  • Candidates Debits from the last 60 days, same amount first and labelled exact match, then everything this month, then search. Nothing is preselected unless the amount and the payee both agree — and even then it is a highlight, not a commitment.
  • Partial Allowed, and it is the common case for a multi-item order. The purchase keeps whatever the refund does not cancel, and its caption reads “partly refunded · ₹800 of ₹2,000”.
  • Arithmetic The credit leaves income. The debit leaves totalSpent up to the refunded amount. Both leave the budget. “Left our accounts” keeps both, because both really did move — which is the same distinction the Summary already draws for savings and self-transfers.
  • Reversible Undo on the snackbar, Unlink on either entry afterwards. A link is a claim about two rows, and a claim you cannot withdraw is one people will not make.
  • Syncs As a field on both transactions, so the link travels and both phones agree about the month’s spending. Two people linking the same credit to different purchases resolves last-write-wins on the Lamport clock, like every other edit.

17 · Lock gate

9:41

Ours is locked

Unlock with your fingerprint, face or screen lock.

LockedLeft-ranged and quiet, like every other screen. A centred padlock with a centred heading is the one shape that says “error”, and being locked is not an error.

  • Authenticators BIOMETRIC_WEAK or DEVICE_CREDENTIAL, never fingerprint alone: not every phone has a sensor, sensors fail with wet or cold hands, and a lock you can get permanently stuck behind is worse than no lock. The PIN is always a way in.
  • Re-lock After 60 seconds in the background. Locking on every glance away makes an app people open ten times a day unusable, and a short grace period is the difference between a feature that stays on and one that gets switched off.
  • Never strand If the hardware cannot satisfy the prompt at all, the gate opens rather than closes. A user must never be locked out of their own data by a capability check.
  • Cancelled Staying locked with a retry button, rather than closing the app out from under the user. That is this screen’s whole reason to exist — the prompt itself is a system sheet and can be dismissed.
