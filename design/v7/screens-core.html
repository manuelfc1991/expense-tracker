The budget knows what day it is

New in v7, and the highest-priority finding in docs/REVIEW.md. On the morning that review was written, Home read 74% used, ₹9,710 left, meter green — on the 6th of a 31-day month. Straight-line pace would be about 19%. The screen said nothing, because nothing in the budget path took the date as an input: fraction = spent / budget, and BudgetAlerter’s thresholds are a flat listOf(100, 80) that fire identically on the 3rd and the 28th. Telling a household whether it is on course is the entire job of a budget, and it was the one question this could not answer.

Why the obvious fix is the wrong one

Dividing the budget by days elapsed would cry wolf every month in this household. The daily bars for August show ₹16,955.79 on day 3 — 59% of the month’s spending in a single day, which is rent and a card bill landing together. Any linear pace model reports a household that pays rent on the 1st as catastrophically overspent for the first fortnight, every single month. An alert that is wrong every month is an alert people switch off.

Pace the discretionary money, not the total

The figure is (budgetLeft − stillCommitted) ÷ daysRemaining. Rent on the 1st never triggers it, because rent was committed and is now paid — and MonthlyAggregator.committedRemaining() already excludes anything whose date has passed. So the line answers the question a household actually asks: what can I spend today without missing the cap. Every input already exists; RecurringDetector finds what repeats and Affordability already reasons about commitments.

It also becomes the honest thing to alert on. BudgetAlerter stops firing at flat percentages and fires when the daily allowance itself falls through a floor or goes negative — which is late in a bad month and never in a normal one.

Why the filter line cannot scroll away

The old row left the selected chip highlighted among the others, which fails in a way that is easy to miss: filter to Rent, scroll the strip back to the left, and the screen is indistinguishable from an unfiltered one that happens to be short. Hidden rows then read as absent rows, which is the worst thing a filter can do to a ledger. The line sits where the chips were, states what is being shown and how much it comes to, and carries its own way out. Tapping its body reopens the sheet, because having decided to filter, the next thing you usually want is a different filter rather than none.

04 · Budgets

Purpose

Set one cap, and optionally a cap per category. Measured on the same ruler Home uses, so “68%” means the same thing on both screens.

  • Ruler vs meter The overall budget gets the tick scale, because it is the one you check against a decision. The per-category rows get plain meters — a dozen tick scales stacked up stops reading as measurement and starts reading as texture.
  • Bar colour A category keeps its own hue until it is in trouble, then takes the semantic one: amber past 80%, red past 100%. A red bar has to mean “over” everywhere, so a category whose own hue happens to be red cannot own that meaning.
  • Components App bar · display-large figure · ruler · label-over-value ×3 · tape header · category rows with avatar + meter · inline budget editor · confirm dialogs
  • Interactions
    • A category row expands into an editor in place, so you never lose your position in a list of sixteen.
    • Whole rupees only — the digit filter means the keyboard cannot produce paise, and a budget with paise in it is a number nobody chose.
    • “Clear this budget” sits below the Cancel/Save pair, not beside it: removing a cap is not the third of three equal choices, and it is confirmed, because the label sits directly under Save and the two do opposite things.
    • “Reset all budgets” is confirmed separately — it drops the overall cap and every category at once, on both phones, and retyping them is not one number but all of them.
  • NavTab 4. Reached also from Home’s “Set one”, with the same nav options so the back stack does not grow a second Budgets entry each time.
  • Improved
    • Editor fields are 48dp and the amount is displayMedium rather than displayLarge, so a six-figure budget no longer overflows.
    • A category with no limit shows a hairline track rather than nothing, so every row has the same height and the list stops jumping as limits are added.
    • Bottom padding drops from 96dp to 40dp — nothing floats over this list, so 96dp was a screen-height of blank below the last panel.

05 · Settings

v7 picks one layout

The current build ships both a one-page scroll and a five-row index, with a chip behind the developer unlock to switch between them — indecision shipped as a preference. v7 keeps the index, because five rows that each report their own state is a status report as well as a menu, and adds a search field, because a 1,669-line settings screen is not navigable by scrolling.

Search settings
Needs you Notification access is off — no expenses are being read

The database on this phone is encrypted with a key held in the Android Keystore. Bluetooth sync is encrypted end to end. Sheet sync is not — it writes plain text, so that you can read and fix the sheet yourself. Your original bank messages are stripped before anything is written there.

IndexEach row reports its own state, so the menu doubles as a status report.

Purpose

Nearly every switch here has a consequence worth a sentence — what sheet sync costs you in privacy, what nearby sync costs in battery — so the prose is the point and the control is the footnote. That inverts the usual preference-list shape, where the caption is a grey afterthought under the title.

  • Needs you Drawn only when something is actually broken, and absent entirely when nothing is — a banner that is always there stops being read. Amber rather than red: nothing is lost, something is merely not running.

    This exists because every failure this app has actually had was a permission that was off while everything else looked fine: POST_NOTIFICATIONS never requested, notification access never granted, and the overlay permission, which Android can revoke at any time without telling anyone. A screen that only reveals that once you scroll to the right section will not catch it.
  • Five pages
    • Household — who lives here, the invite QR and code, delete requests.
    • Sync — sheet URL behind a disclosure, Bluetooth toggle, sync now.
    • What becomes an entry — SMS / Notifications / Manual, the tracking start date, rescan, rules, parser tester.
    • When a payment happens — the notification and the popup, which are one decision made twice and were five sections apart.
    • This app — theme, contrast, accent, app lock, version and update.
  • Components App bar · search · compact notice · panel · panel rows with pill / switch / spinner · disclosure rows · segmented buttons · accent swatches · QR plate · date picker · linear progress · confirm dialogs
  • Interactions
    • Search filters rows across all five pages and shows the page each match lives on.
    • Back closes an open page before it closes the screen.
    • Permission rows are drawn whether or not the permission is granted. Shown only while missing, a row disappears the moment it starts working — so there is no way to check it later and no way back to the system page to change your mind. Both of these permissions are revocable outside the app, which makes “currently granted” a fact worth being able to look up.
    • Seven taps on the version, then one on the household code, unlocks developer mode, and it counts down out loud from four remaining. A hidden sequence with no feedback is indistinguishable from one that does not work.
  • Nav Tab 5. Out to: Rules, Parser tester, Sheet setup, QR scanner, Delete requests, Backup, Trash. Each of those is on the Flows page.
  • Improved
    • One layout instead of two behind a flag.
    • A search field, which is the only realistic way into a screen this size.
    • Trash and Backup lift out of “This app” into their own panel — they are the answers to “I deleted something” and “the phone is gone”, not housekeeping beside a version number.
    • Theme / contrast use segmented buttons rather than chip rows, which is what MD3 has for a small exclusive set.

Paste the Apps Script URL from your Google Sheet. Use the same URL on your partner’s phone and you’ll both see every expense, wherever you are.

Connected — “Ours household ledger”

For a sheet you recreated or cleared. The phone otherwise believes it already sent everything, and only new expenses would appear.

64 expenses from before 12 April 2026 will not be sent. Retiring a month keeps it off the sheet and off the other phone, not just off this screen.

Anyone with this URL can read and change your expenses — treat it like a password. Amounts, merchants and account tails are stored in the clear; the original bank messages are stripped before they are sent.
