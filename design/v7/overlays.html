Sheets, dialogs and overlays

Everything that appears over a screen: four bottom sheets, nine dialogs, the popup that runs when the app is closed, and the notification. Together these are where most of the app’s real work happens — a payment is categorised from a sheet or a notification far more often than from any screen.

Which container, and why

ContainerForRule
Bottom sheetA task with more than one field, or a grid. Modal, 28dp top corners, grabber, dismissible by drag or scrim. Never hosts a live text field if it can appear unbidden — see the capture sheet below.
DialogOne question, or one irreversible confirmation. Names the object it is about. A confirmation that does not say which row is answered from memory of which one was tapped, which is the mistake it exists to catch.
SnackbarSomething happened, and it may be undone. One per batch. Clears the nav bar and the FAB. No action when there is genuinely nothing to undo.
NotificationA payment landed while the app was closed. Three one-tap categories, because Android renders at most three actions and silently drops the rest.
Overlay activityThe same prompt, over another app. Only when the app is not in the foreground, only when the row is uncategorised, and only while the overlay permission holds. Never replaces the notification.

Category pickerThe same grid as the detail screen, the add sheet and the filter — one list, one order, one place each category lives.

Why the capture sheet cannot host a text field

It appears unbidden, over whatever you were reading, a second after the bank messages you. Raising a keyboard inside it would shove the whole sheet up the screen the moment it arrived. So naming and noting open a dialog instead — but the line they open from is drawn identically to the one on the add sheet, because to the person using it there is no difference between the two.

The add sheet has the opposite constraint: you asked for it, so its keyboard opens immediately, on the amount. The amount is the only field that cannot be guessed, corrected later from a message, or left out — an expense with an amount and nothing else is still a true row, while one with a payee and no amount is not a row at all. So it is also the only thing Save waits for.

What each sheet asks for

  • AddAmount (required), payee, category, when, household/personal, note. An unnamed row saves as “Cash”, which is a truer answer than an empty payee column that reads as a parsing failure six months later.
  • CaptureOnly what the bank left out. A named merchant needs a category and nothing else; an unnamed debit leads with the question nobody else can answer and offers the destination account, because naming that once names every payment to it.
  • FilterEvery category with its count, including the zeroes. The count is the answer to “did anything land under Health this month”, and hiding an empty category would send you looking elsewhere for something that was never there.
  • PickerOne tap, no confirm. It is used dozens of times in a Sort session and a confirm step would double the work.

New in v7

The add sheet gets a When row. Without it, a cash lunch entered in the evening lands in the wrong hour and — entered after midnight — the wrong day’s subtotal. “Now” stays the default, so the common case still costs nothing.

Dialogs

Nine of them. Four ask a question, five confirm something that cannot be casually undone. Every confirmation names its object and states honestly what is recoverable — a confirmation that overstates the damage is one people learn to dismiss unread, which costs exactly the protection it was added for.

Popup over another appThe same prompt as the capture sheet, in a transparent-window activity. Same content, different container — and they must not become different prompts.

Four conditions, and two cannot be cached

All of these must hold before the popup launches:

  1. The popup is switched on.
  2. The row is one nobody has categorised. A payment the app already understands has nothing to ask, and a popup that appears anyway is just an interruption.
  3. Android still grants the overlay permission — revocable in system settings at any time, without the app being told.
  4. No screen of this app is already in front. With Ours open, the in-app sheet is showing the same prompt and a second window over it would be absurd.

The launch can still be refused — the permission is necessary, not sufficient — and Android throws rather than returning false when it does. So the call is guarded, and the notification is never replaced: if the launch fails, or the popup is swiped away undecided, the notification is still in the tray.

The prompt is supposed to fade away

When the app is confident about a payee, no category buttons are shown and no popup appears. As the rules accumulate, this whole surface should be seen less and less. A prompt on every purchase forever is a tax, not a feature.
