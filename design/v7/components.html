Three of these are new

The current build has a success state everywhere, a skeleton on Home only, and a one-line text empty state. It has no offline design at all and no error surface outside a red sentence buried inside a Settings disclosure. For an app whose whole premise is two phones staying in step, “what is stale and what is merely late” is not a corner case; it is the normal condition of the second phone.

StateTriggerRule
LoadingFirst read of a month; a rescan in flight. Skeleton in the shape of the content, at the real row height. Never a centred spinner over a blank page — a spinner does not say what it is waiting for. Anything under 200ms shows nothing at all, because a flash is worse than a wait.
EmptyNothing recorded, or a filter that matches nothing. Icon, a sentence about the world, and a body line about why that is fine. An action only when there is genuinely one to take — an empty Trash offers nothing, an empty search offers “clear the filter”.
SuccessThe normal case. The one on the far left. Everything else on these pages is this state.
OfflineNo network, or last sync older than the sync interval. A 36dp ribbon under the app bar, never a blocking dialog. It states what is certainly current (this phone’s own SMS) and what may be missing (the other phone since a named time). Figures are never hidden or greyed — they are correct, just possibly incomplete.
ErrorA sync, restore, update or parse that failed. Named cause, one fix, and the data left visible beneath it. Red is reserved for this and for loss; something merely not running is amber.

A behaviour change worth arguing about

The current add button vanishes the instant the list starts moving, so that it can never cover an amount — the amount column being the whole layout. The cost is that the only way to add an expense is unavailable during exactly the gesture people use while looking for it, and it reappears under the thumb when the fling stops.

v7 shrinks it to the 40dp variant on scroll-down and restores it on scroll-up or at rest. The list keeps its bottom padding so the last row still clears the corner, so the original concern is met without the control ever going away.

Lists and rows

Statement entry — every variant
Wed 5 Aug₹2,955.19

Selection tints the row and marks its left edge rather than adding a checkbox column — a checkbox would push every payee, time and amount sideways the instant selection began, so the list you were reading would rearrange itself under your thumb at exactly the moment you were aiming at something. The last row is a Trash entry: dimmed amount, because it is in no total, and its caption counts down in red under three days.

No swipe actions. Deliberately.

This row used to be a swipe-to-dismiss box — left to delete, right to categorise — and it had to go. The gesture that dismisses a row and the gesture that scrolls a list start out identical, and a list exists to be scrolled. A thumb travelling up the screen at a slight angle was deleting entries by accident, at a threshold already set to 55% of the row width. That is not tunable. Deleting is on the detail screen and in the selection toolbar; a category can be set from five places. Each needs a deliberate tap, which is the right price for changing a row you did not open.

The selection bar was a mis-tap trap

It is currently four identical 9sp uppercase words — All · Categorize · Delete · Done — with Delete third, between two harmless ones, each tapping a bare text label with no target padding. The batch it destroys can include hand-made entries that exist in no backup.

v7 makes them 48dp icon buttons, moves Delete to the far end where a thumb reaching for “done” cannot find it, gives it the error colour, and keeps the confirm dialog and the undo. Same four actions, three separate defences.

Bottom navigation — with an active indicator and a badge

The indicator uses secondaryContainer, not the accent. That satisfies both concerns at once: the selected tab is unmistakable, and the accent stays unique to actions, which was the reason the current build has no indicator at all. Labels are 12sp sentence case rather than 9sp uppercase — “ACTIVITY” tracked out at 9sp is the hardest text in the app to read and it is the one you navigate by.

The badge on Activity is the untagged count. It exists nowhere today, so a household with 94 unsorted rows has no idea until it happens to open Home. The dot on Settings means something needs attention — a revoked permission, a delete request waiting.

Measurement

Ruler — discrete ticks, because a budget is “this many out of that many”
68% used
112% — over fills the whole scale in the warning colour
no budget set — an empty scale, not a filled one

A solid fill says “roughly this much”. Ticks say “this many out of that many”, and it borrows the vernacular of a printed scale, which is the material the whole interface is made of. A ruler that ran past its own end would just look broken, so over-budget fills completely and changes colour.

Not a donut: a donut is only readable when its slices are of comparable size, which household spending never is — one category at 95% is a circle with a notch in it. Scaling against the largest category instead of the month’s total would make the top bar full-width by definition, so ₹3,233 of a ₹3,233 month and ₹8,412 of a ₹22,000 one would draw the identical bar. The name and the amount sit on the same line as the length, which is what keeps the colour from being load-bearing.
