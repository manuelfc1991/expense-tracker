The unlock screen

The one screen in the app with nothing to show. It is also, for anyone who turns the lock on, the screen they see more often than any other — several times a day, always before they have got what they came for. It currently spends that moment on a padlock, a sentence and a button on an empty field.

This redesign spends it on the statement itself, redacted. The month is laid out exactly as it always is — same rows, same hairlines, same right-hand amount column — with a bar of ink standing where every figure and every name would be. It is recognisably your ledger and it discloses nothing, which is precisely what a lock is.

c · Light (locked to light)Ink on paper, which is what redaction has always been. The bars are onSurface at 13%, so they take the theme with them rather than being a grey that only works on one.

  • What is redacted Every figure, every merchant name, every date, and the category colour. The dot is a square of the same ink, never the category hue — “you cannot see the amount, but it was Health” is still a disclosure, and it is the one most people would mind most. This is the only place in the package where a category mark is drawn without its colour.
  • What is not The labels: “Spent this month”, “This month”, the wordmark. They describe the app’s structure, not the household’s money, and stripping them would leave an abstract pattern rather than a recognisable statement. The row count is not real either — always six visible, never “you had four transactions today”.
  • Bar widths are fixed Authored, not derived. A bar as wide as the figure behind it would leak the order of magnitude: ₹9,00,000 and ₹90 are three characters apart. Six rows, one authored pattern, identical on every launch — the shape is of a statement, not this one.
  • Still, never shimmering .redact is deliberately not .skel. Shimmer means “this is arriving”; a lock screen would be promising exactly what it is refusing, and the animation would run for as long as the phone sat on the table. See the note in ours.css §4.
  • Nothing new below the fold The redacted field is the flexible row; the sentence and button are pinned. On the 620dp window the field shows four rows instead of eight and nothing else moves — the button stays the same distance from the thumb.
  • Unchanged from the built screen Left rag, the sentence verbatim, the filled button, one accent. This is a re-composition, not a new voice. Being locked is still not an error and the screen still does not say it is.

Too many attempts

Your fingerprint is off for 28 seconds. Your screen lock still works.

Temporarily locked outAmber, not red: nothing here is wrong, something is merely not running yet. The way in becomes the primary button, and the way that cannot work becomes the disabled one — not the reverse.

  • The bug this fixes onAuthenticationError ignores its code. On ERROR_LOCKOUT the user lands on a screen offering one button, which reopens a prompt that fails instantly, with nothing on screen saying it will keep failing for half a minute. The redesign reads the code; that is the whole change.
  • Which codes ERROR_LOCKOUT (30s) and ERROR_LOCKOUT_PERMANENT (until the screen lock is used) get this state. ERROR_USER_CANCELED, ERROR_NEGATIVE_BUTTON and ERROR_CANCELED get §19b — a dismissal is not a failure and must not be dressed as one.
  • Amber, not red The house rule: red is for a figure that is wrong. No figure is wrong here. The same rule that keeps “sync not running” amber keeps this amber.
  • The countdown is the honest part A live second count, because “try again later” makes people tap repeatedly and each tap on some OEM builds extends the window. Naming the number ends the tapping.
  • Permanent lockout Same layout, no countdown: “Your fingerprint is off until you use your screen lock.” The primary button is unchanged, which is the point — the PIN is always a way in, and on this screen it is now the biggest thing on it.

Mid-transition (a frame, not a state)Bars fade out top first, content fades in behind them. Nothing translates, nothing scales, nothing waits on a layout pass — the ledger was always there.

  • Timing --dur-medium-2 (300ms), --ease-standard, staggered 24ms per row from the top. Total under 450ms — inside the package’s “a ledger is a thing you check, not watch” ceiling.
  • Why top-down The figure people came for is at the top. It clears first and is readable before the animation has finished, so the stagger never costs anyone a moment.
  • No reverse Re-locking after 60 seconds in the background is not animated. The screen is composed before it is ever visible, and animating into a lock would show, for 300ms, the content being locked away — a leak with a fade on it.
  • Reduced motion prefers-reduced-motion cross-fades the whole field once, no stagger. The bars must still go — they are the state, not the decoration.

22 · Carried over unchanged

None of this is up for renegotiation by a visual redesign. It is repeated here because the reasons are load-bearing and live in AppLock.kt, not in a mockup.

  • Authenticators BIOMETRIC_WEAK or DEVICE_CREDENTIAL, never fingerprint alone: not every phone has a sensor, sensors fail with wet or cold hands, and a lock you can get permanently stuck behind is worse than no lock. The PIN is always a way in.
  • Re-lock After 60 seconds in the background. Locking on every glance away makes an app people open ten times a day unusable, and a short grace period is the difference between a feature that stays on and one that gets switched off.
  • Never strand If the hardware cannot satisfy the prompt at all, the gate opens rather than closes. A user must never be locked out of their own data by a capability check.
  • Dismissal keeps the app open Staying locked with a retry, rather than closing the app out from under the user. The prompt is a system sheet and can be dismissed; §19b is what is behind it when it is.
  • Cost of the change
    • One new composable — a redacted StatementEntry — plus one when over the error code in onAuthenticationError.
    • No new permission, no new dependency, no new stored state, and nothing read out of the database: the bars are authored, so the locked screen never touches a transaction to draw itself.
