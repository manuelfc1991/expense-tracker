The design system

A bank SMS is a machine-printed statement line: fixed pitch, amount flush right, a hairline between entries. That is the material this interface is made of, and v7 does not replace it. What v7 does is put it on Material 3 — proper colour roles, a 4dp grid, the MD3 shape and motion scales, real touch targets — so the house style stops being a set of one-off decisions and becomes a system that can be handed to Figma or to another developer without a translator.

Every colour pair on this page was computed, not judged. Where a figure fails, it says so.

Six principles

These are decisions, not aspirations. Each one rules something out, which is what makes it usable in a review: you can point at a proposal and say it breaks number four.

PrincipleWhat it meansWhat it rules out
1. The row is a printed line Mark, name over caption, amount flush right in one shared column, hairline underneath. Amounts carry no ₹ per row — once the column is aligned, the column is the unit. A card per transaction. Boxing every line of a statement is precisely what a statement does not do.
2. One accent, one action Exactly one accent-filled thing per screen. Green means money arriving, amber means something is not running, red means a figure is wrong or lost. A second primary button. A “Save” and a “Done” on the same screen makes both stop meaning “do this”.
3. Label above, never beside The caption sits over its figure so the eye lands on the number and reads the caption only if it needs to. Inline Budget: ₹32,000, which forces both to be read in sequence.
4. Structure by hairline, not by shadow Depth comes from 1px rules and stepped near-black surfaces. Shadow is spent only on things that genuinely float: FAB, dialog, sheet. Elevation as decoration. A raised card for content that is not raised.
5. Say what is missing, never “no data” “Nothing yet this month” is a fact about the month. Empty, error and offline all name the thing and offer the way out. “No data”, “Error 500”, a bare spinner with no idea what it is waiting for.
6. Colour is never the only carrier Every coloured bar, chip and pill also prints its name and its number. Twelve category hues cannot be told apart by everyone and are not asked to be. A legend-only chart. Red/green as the sole difference between two states.

Colour

MD3 role names throughout, so the scheme drops into darkColorScheme() and lightColorScheme() without a mapping layer. Light is not an inversion of dark: a statement is ink on paper, so paper is the original and it gets its own validated hues. Dropping the dark values onto white fails contrast and turns muddy, the greens and ambers worst of all.

Why there are two blues

The brand accent #2F5BFF is a surface colour: white sits on it at 5.2:1 and it makes a good button. As text on near-black it is only 3.80:1 — and the app sets accent text everywhere: “Sort”, “Edit”, “See all”, “Put back”, every MicroLabel(color = Ours.accent). All of it currently fails AA.

So v7 splits the role, which is exactly what MD3’s fixed roles are for. primary is the accent as text and icon — tone 80 on dark, tone 30 on paper. primaryFixed is the brand blue, unchanged in both themes, used only where something is filled. One accent, two jobs, and both legible.

The twelve category hues

Hue carries the meaning — debt reads red, growth green, travel teal — and is anchored within ±9° of that meaning. What v7 changes is that lightness is allowed to vary. The current palette holds every category inside one lightness band on purpose, and that is exactly what makes them collapse for a colour-blind reader: once hue information is gone, lightness is nearly all the separation left.

What this palette costs — stated, not hidden

Separation was bought with lightness, and lightness is not free. Two honest costs:

Some hues drift from their meaning. On paper, Bills is #3F3312 — a dark olive-brown rather than the amber “utility” was meant to read as, and Transport is #0C2E46, nearly navy rather than a road blue. The hue angles are still right to within 9°; what has gone is the saturation and lightness that made the association obvious. On paper every category ink is dark, which is at least consistent with a statement, but Bills no longer looks like a bill.

Some dark values run brighter than the original band. Education #C9DA5C and Fun #E048E7 sit above the L .51–.62 band the current palette holds itself to, and that band exists for a reason the source states plainly: a brighter pass “would have glared on OLED and fought the text”. These two are the ones to watch on a real phone at night. If either proves uncomfortable, drop its lightness and re-run the validator — the floor to keep is ΔE 10 across all 66 pairs and 4.5:1 on every surface, not any particular hex.

Both costs are worth paying, because the thing they buy is a palette a colour-blind reader can use, and the alternative was a palette where Food and Education were the same colour. But they are costs, and the next person to touch these values should know they were chosen against a measured objective rather than by eye.

Typography

Two families. Noto Sans Mono for every number and every small-caps caption — it is the one common grotesque mono that draws ₹ (U+20B9) itself, where JetBrains Mono and Google’s Roboto Mono subset both omit it and would set the rupee sign from a fallback face beside the digits, in the 44sp figure that carries the whole hierarchy of the home screen. Roboto for prose. Tabular figures everywhere a number appears, or the right-hand amount column visibly jitters as digits change and stops lining up at all.

The one breaking change

The micro-label goes from 9sp to 11sp. It is the most-used style in the app — it captions every figure on every screen — and 9sp is below MD3’s labelSmall floor and hard for a lot of readers. The wide tracking is what signals “this is a caption”, not the smallness, so nothing is lost by giving it the extra two points. Expect roughly 4–6px of extra height per captioned figure; the screens on the following pages are drawn at the new size and still fit.

One zone, not two — the latent bug in docs/REVIEW.md §3

All money maths runs in a fixed zone — MonthlyAggregator.ZONE = Asia/Kolkata — and every date the interface formats runs in another, ZoneId.systemDefault(): in Statement.kt, SummaryScreen.kt, TransactionDetailScreen.kt, TrashScreen.kt, SettingsScreen.kt, and most consequentially in the tracking-start date picker, which converts the chosen day locally and then compares it against transactions bucketed into months in IST.

Both phones sit in IST today, so the two agree and nothing is visibly wrong. Take one phone to another zone and the cutoff moves by hours, and Activity’s day headings stop agreeing with the month totals they sit under. ExportManager and BackupManager already hard-code Asia/Kolkata and are consistent; the interface is the odd one out.

The rule: every date in this design — caption, day rule, month stepper, picker, countdown — is rendered in the same zone the money is bucketed in. One constant, used in both places. It is a design rule and not only an implementation detail, because it is what makes a day’s subtotal equal to the sum of the rows printed under it.

The row caption has a 25-character budget

A statement row spends its width on the amount column, so what is left for the caption is narrow and fixed. At 360dp: 360 − 32 (edges) − 32 (mark) − 24 (gaps) − 66 (amount) = 206px, and an 11sp mono character with 1.5px tracking is 8.1px. That is 25 characters, and the caption is one line with ellipsis — so a caption that overflows does not wrap, it silently loses its tail.

Which is worse than it sounds

The tail is where the important part lives. Food · 22 Jul · 11 days left is 28 characters, so a Trash row would have rendered as FOOD · 22 JUL · 11 DA… — clipping the day count, which is the only reason that row has a caption at all. Eleven caption patterns in this package busted the budget before it was measured.

The rule: the mark already states the category, so the caption spends its characters on what the mark cannot say. Drop the category word whenever something more specific needs the room.

ContextCaptionWhy
Today, one personFood · 7:48 am Room to spare, so the category is spelled out.

At a 200% font scale the budget falls to about 12 characters, which no useful caption fits. There the row reflows: the caption moves under the payee at full width and the amount keeps its right edge. That is the only place in this design where a statement row stops being one line.

Icons

Drawn for this app, not borrowed. 24dp grid, single-weight 1.75dp stroke, round caps and joins, 20dp live area. One source of geometry — tools/icons.py — generates both the Android vector drawables and the SVG sprite these pages use, so a glyph in this document is byte-for-byte the glyph that ships. Filled variants exist only for the five selected bottom-nav tabs.

Why the set was redrawn

What it replaces is Bootstrap Icons 1.11.3 — filled silhouettes on a 16px grid, in an app that draws icons at 13–24dp. Two things went wrong at those sizes, and both are visible on a real phone rather than in a preview:

Detail collapsed. The old wastebasket put a lid, a rim, a tapered body and three tick marks into solid fill. At 13dp — the size the Trash row and the delete button actually use — the lid merged into the body and it read as a grey blob. It is the glyph that prompted the whole redraw.

The weight was never consistent. A glyph drawn on a 16 grid and scaled to 24dp carries roughly 1.5× the apparent stroke of one drawn at 24, so a screen mixing category marks with chevrons and status glyphs never looked like one family however carefully the set had been chosen.

Stroke geometry rather than filled outline is the load-bearing decision. The weight becomes one number instead of an emergent property of every shape, and a stroke does not thin as the drawable scales down. Android has supported strokeWidth and strokeColor on <path> since API 21 and this app is minSdk 26, so it costs nothing — Icon(painter, tint = …) applies a colour filter over the rendered drawable, so stroke glyphs tint exactly as the filled ones did.

RuleDetail
Grid24×24 canvas, 20×20 live area — 2dp clear on every side. Circle d19, square 18×18. Terminals land on whole or half units. Anything that breaks a keyline says why, in a comment beside it in tools/icons.py.
Stroke1.75dp, round cap, round join, outer corners 2.0, inner 1.25. One weight across the whole set; no exceptions.
Sizes14dp inside chips and grid cells · 18dp in rows and buttons · 20dp in icon buttons and app bars · 24dp FAB and nav · 32dp empty states and onboarding. Because the source is 24 and stroked, every one of these is the same apparent weight.
Detail budgetAt most two elements in a glyph that will be drawn at 13dp, and 1.5dp of clearance between them. The heart’s pulse trace had to be pulled in and shortened for exactly this reason: at 13dp it was merging with the heart’s own stroke, and a heart-shaped blob reads as “favourite”, not “health”.
TargetsThe glyph is not the target. Every tappable icon sits inside a 48dp square (40dp where two are unavoidably adjacent, never below 24dp).
ColourA category glyph carries its category’s hue everywhere it appears — the avatar on a row, the chip, the grid cell, the Rules header. Everything else is onSurfaceVariant, or primary when it is the action.
MeaningOne glyph, one job. A chevron means “there is more this way” and is never an up/down arrow, because those mean money moved — a real bug this set had, where the disclosure control on Rules was a pair of trend arrows. Savings was likewise a bar chart whose connecting line dipped before it rose, so it read as a crown; it is now a rising arrow, because for that category the only job is “up”.
LabelsEvery icon-only control carries a contentDescription. An icon is never the only way a state is announced.

The rest of the set

Accessibility

The audit found four systematic problems, all of them the kind that never shows up in a screenshot. Each is fixed by a rule rather than by a one-off correction.

ProblemFound inThe rule now
Touch targets Back arrows tap a bare 16dp glyph. The rule-chip delete is 9dp. Bill dismiss is 13dp. The detail-screen delete is a 15dp glyph a thumb’s width from Back. The glyph is never the target. 48dp minimum, 40dp only where two controls are unavoidably adjacent, never below the 24dp WCAG 2.5.8 floor. Destructive controls get 8dp of clear space from anything benign.
Text size The 9sp micro-label, used to caption every figure on every screen. 11sp floor for all text. Nothing in the app is smaller.
Contrast Accent-as-text at 3.80:1. Light-theme captions at 4.25:1 — a failure the source comments already admit. Shopping at 2.90:1, also admitted. Split the accent into primary (text) and primaryFixed (fills). Every role re-derived and measured; the worst text pair in v7 is 5.00:1.
Colour-blindness Food and Education are near-identical to a deuteranope; the source names this and accepts it. Lightness is allowed to vary across the set, which is what restores separation once hue is gone. Validated against all three dichromacies — figures above. Name and amount still print beside every bar regardless.
Announcements Selection count, sync state and untagged count are visual only. Live-region announcements for selection changes and sync results; state pills carry their text, not just their colour; the untagged count is a nav-bar badge with a spoken label.

Dynamic type

The app must survive the system font scale to 200%. Two consequences the current layout would not tolerate: the shared amount column has to keep its right edge (it grows, it does not wrap), and the three-figure BUDGET / USED / LEFT row has to reflow to two lines rather than crush. Both are specified on the Core screens page.

Handoff

The tokens as they land in each tool. This page is the source; the two blocks below are derived from it.

Figma

  • Two colour variable modes — Light and Dark — on one collection, named exactly as the MD3 roles above (surface, onSurfaceVariant, primaryFixed …). Never a raw hex in a layer.
  • Category hues in a second collection, category/food … so a bar can be rebound without touching the semantic roles.
  • Number variables for the spacing scale, so auto-layout gaps are space-3 rather than 12.
  • Text styles named for the role, not the appearance: display/large, not Mono 44 Bold.
  • Components with variants for every state on the Components page — including disabled and error, which are the two that get skipped and then get improvised in code.
